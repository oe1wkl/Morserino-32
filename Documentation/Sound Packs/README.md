# Sound packs for the M32 Pocket

Replacement sounds for the two signals that tell you whether your answer was right or
wrong — in the Echo Trainer, and in the games. Five pairs, pick one, or use them as a
starting point for your own.

**These are for the M32 Pocket only.** The classic Morserino-32 does not play sound files.

## Installing a pack

Each folder holds two files. Upload them with the **Configuration Tool**, under exactly
these names:

| file in the pack | upload as |
|---|---|
| `success.mp3` | `/sounds/success.mp3` |
| `error.mp3` | `/sounds/error.mp3` |

The Morserino plays them instead of its built-in signals from then on. Delete them and
the built-in signals come back. You can mix packs — a `success.mp3` from one and an
`error.mp3` from another — and you can install just one of the two.

## The packs

| pack | success | error | character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **fanfare** | rising major triad, brass | falling minor triad, reed | closest to the built-in signals, with a bit more of an occasion made of it |
| **bells** | two handbell strikes, rising | two handbell strikes, falling | bright and ringing, clearly audible across a room |
| **marimba** | three wooden notes, rising | two wooden notes, falling | dry and short — good if you find a ringing tone intrusive between repetitions |
| **chime** | soft glass, rising fourth | soft glass, falling fourth | slow and quiet, for a quiet room or if any beep startles you |
| **arcade** | fast chiptune arpeggio | descending chiptune | for the games |

Every success sound **rises** in pitch and every error sound **falls**. That is the
Morserino's convention and it is worth keeping in anything you make yourself: you should
be able to tell the two apart without listening for detail, from across the room, while
thinking about something else.

## Volume

These are deliberately quieter than a normally-mastered audio file: each is matched to the
level of the Morserino's own built-in signals, which sit **below** the CW you are
practising — the error sounds by about 6 dB, the success sounds by about 9. That is on
purpose, and the difference between the two is on purpose as well: you hear the success
sound far more often, and it tells you nothing you did not already know, so it is the more
discreet of the pair. A signal that is louder than the Morse code itself gets tiring fast
in a long session.

They follow the **Tone Volume** setting like every other sound, so turning the Morserino up
turns them up too.

## Making your own

Two things to respect, and one that is easy to get wrong:

1. **Keep the peak level at or below −1 dBFS.** A file normalised to the full 0 dBFS will
   distort, because the MP3 decoder runs out of headroom before the sound chip does.
2. **That is a ceiling, not a target.** A file mastered up against −1 dBFS comes out around
   6 dB *louder* than the built-in signals — louder than your CW. If you want it to sit
   where these do, aim a good deal lower; the files here peak between −12 and −2 dBFS.
3. **Do not go too low in pitch.** The Pocket's built-in loudspeaker radiates very little
   below about 300 Hz. A deep, serious-sounding error tone that is perfectly clear on your
   computer can be nearly inaudible on the device. Every error sound in this pack sits at
   or above 290 Hz and gets its weight from harmonics rather than from a low fundamental —
   on headphones you will not notice the difference, on the built-in speaker you will.

MP3, 44.1 kHz stereo, 128 kbit/s. Keep them short: half a second to a second. They are
generated by `devdocs/signal-tones/make_jingles.py`, which also measures what it made.
